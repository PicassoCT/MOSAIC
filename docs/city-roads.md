# City roads and plot addresses

City roads is enabled by default. A faint blue road hint reaches **0.25 opacity** at camera height 2450 above terrain and smoothly vanishes below 650. Pure street names remain at close zoom; small 11-pixel labels use collision checks and a lower label budget at distance. No bloom or emissive lighting is added. Geometry and sector dashes are cached once and released on shutdown.

Two generation paths share `scripts/lib_city_roads.lua`:

- **Generated by game:** wait for the actual initial house plots, rasterize their clearance and terrain exclusions, and extract smoothed street centrelines only around occupied neighbourhoods. No full-map grid is drawn through unoccupied outskirts. Continuous tangent-compatible routes retain one name through junctions. Names contain no internal IDs or coordinates.
- **Hand-authored maps:** explicit `mosaic/roads.lua` takes priority. Terrain explicitly named Street/Road/Asphalt is traced when present. Dhubai's compiled terrain-type map is actually all zero, despite its declared Street type: `scripts/city_roads/dhubai.lua` instead contains asphalt centrelines recovered from the real SMF minimap, not invented grid lanes. `tools/bake_dhubai_roads.py` documents the exact source hash, neutral-asphalt selection and airport apron exclusion. The atlas is limited to LastDayOfDubai/Dhubai names at 8192 square. Other painted maps with no road metadata use plot inference; their painted roads cannot be recovered from height alone.
- **Generated by map:** CityLights exports projected, clipped OSM highway polylines and keeps source names and imported addresses. Connected equal-name ways share an address identity. The renderer rounds corners within half a road width; it does not replace the map geometry.

The recovered Dhubai atlas was visually compared with its native minimap. Raster extraction has 32-world-unit resolution and may preserve minor asphalt-detail branches; it is an approximation, not surveyed road data. Inferred streets are orientation hints, not new physical roads or pathfinding changes.

The old TacticalGrid renderer yields to this unified overlay. Sectors are fixed 1024-unit areas: columns A, B, C from world X=0; rows 1, 2, 3 from world Z=0. Labels and dashed boundaries are fainter than roads and disappear close up. A small cursor-ground sector readout, house tooltip sector and radio chatter use the **same** shared resolver. Camera rotation does not rename a sector. For example: “B3, Market Street, east of the mosque.” This names a useful location without placing coordinates inside street names. `WG.CityOrientation.SectorAt` and `StreetAt` expose the cosmetic lookup to other UI.

The map contract is a data-only Lua table:

```lua
return {schema=1, generation="map", roads={
  {id="way/123/1", name="Market Street", width=32,
   points={{400,600},{1200,600},{1800,900}}}
}}
```

All coordinates are Spring world X/Z units. IDs must be unique, widths are 1–128 units, and coordinates are rounded to integer units. Limits are 4096 road pieces and 65536 points. The exported widths are class-based display estimates, not surveyed physical widths. Street names retain UTF-8, with control characters removed.

Each house belongs to the original **plot**, keyed by rounded world X/Z, rather than a unit ID. The initial set is sorted along its street; opposite sides receive odd/even numbers. Imported `addr:street` and `addr:housenumber` take precedence. Generated numbers skip imported numbers. An imported street without matching geometry remains an address-only street instead of being renamed.

The synced service publishes `city_plot_key`, `city_plot_x`, `city_plot_z`, `city_street_id`, `city_street_name`, `city_house_number`, `city_number_source`, and `city_generation` as public unit rules parameters. The tooltip includes the same street and number. Repeated calls and destroyed-house replacements retain the plot's address. A genuinely new plot after initial city completion gets a coordinate suffix, so it cannot renumber existing houses or collide through construction order. Imported duplicate numbers are retained as source data.

CityLights placements may supply an `address` record with these fields. Map and game service implementations must match. The generated map owns the authoritative road network and initial addresses; MOSAIC manages tooltip integration and rebuilding.

Old address code mixed half-grid and world coordinates, chose some street directions randomly and incremented a counter keyed only by display name. This update removes those dependencies, fixes shop array bounds and double-scaled shop coordinates, makes shop descriptors deterministic, and sorts/falls back safely when choosing truck destination names.

Run `lua5.1 tests/city_roads_test.lua`, `lua5.1 tests/city_roads_organic_test.lua` and `lua5.1 tests/city_arcologies_test.lua`. The first suite forbids RNG use and checks reversed geometry/plot order, connected street IDs, UTF-8, imported numbers, repeated registration, rebuilt houses, rules-state recovery, zoom fade, label collision checks and GL cleanup. The second checks the existing city and arcology lifecycle. CI executes Lua 5.1 on Linux and Windows via Lupa.

Recoil visual rendering and multiplayer use still require an engine smoke test. In particular, inspect lane alignment in each culture, labels at several zoom levels, map-widget loading, and a destroyed/rebuilt house on both game-generated and CityLights maps.
